Graph-Aware Review Coordinator

How ChessSRS traverses repertoires as DAGs, manages transpositions, applies lapse contagion, and schedules review queues.

Source in app repo: docs/review-2-graph-aware-coordinator.md
App version: v1.4.0
Licence: GPL-3.0

1. The Repertoire as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)

Individual games are trees, but an entire chess opening repertoire is a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). Different move orders frequently converge to identical board positions (transpositions).

Generic flashcard engines treat every node independently. If a player practices 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4, and separately reaches the same Nimzo-Indian through an English move order 1.c4 e6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 Bb4, a naive system would schedule them as two separate cards. The user would be tested twice on the identical position!

ChessSRS bridges this gap with its Graph-Aware Review Coordinator (lib/src/domain/review/graph_coordinator.dart).

2. Transpositions & Canonical Position Identity

Every RepertoireDecision is canonicalized on FEN4 (piece placement, active color, castling availability, and en-passant target) combined with the expected repertoire move in UCI notation:

Canonical Key CalculationDart
// Half-move clock and full-move counter are stripped: they do not alter legal moves or tactical patterns.
final canonicalKey = sha1('$fen4|$expectedMoveUci');

State Sharing Mechanics

  • Single Shared Knowledge State: If two tree nodes across different chapters share the same canonicalKey, they map to the exact same ReviewState record in SQLite.
  • Bi-directional Updates: Answering the move correctly in Chapter A updates the stability and due date for that position across Chapter B and Chapter C automatically.
  • Contradiction Detection: If the same FEN4 occurs in two studies with conflicting expected moves, ChessSRS flags this as a repertoire contradiction rather than training interfering habits.

3. Upstream Lapse Contagion Decay

What should happen to descendant moves when you make a mistake on move 4?

  • Naive Approach 1: Reset the entire branch to Day 1. (Demoralizing; produces giant review backlogs from a single slip).
  • Naive Approach 2: Leave child nodes untouched. (Dangerous; if you forgot move 4, you cannot reach move 8 in a real game).

ChessSRS implements exponentially decaying lapse contagion, softly nudging descendant nodes without resetting their repetition history:

Lapse Contagion Equation
S_child' = S_child · (1 - λ0 · e^(-(depth - 1) / τ)) D_child' = clip(D_child + β · λ0 · e^(-(depth - 1) / τ), 1.0, 10.0)
Defaults: λ0 = 0.18 (max 18% stability haircut on immediate child), τ = 1.5 plies (contagion dissipates rapidly with depth), β = 0.6 (small difficulty nudge).

The child's nextDueAt is pulled forward proportionally, bringing the downstream line back into review sooner while leaving its active repetition count intact.

4. Auto-Traversal Exposure Credit (INV-024)

When testing a deep variation at move 12, the player is guided through the initial moves 1 through 11. Passive exposure reinforces visual familiarity, but is not active recall.

ChessSRS grants prefix moves an auto-traversal exposure bump:

S' = S · (1 + ε), where ε = 0.08 (+8% stability)
Strict guardrails prevent abuse:
• Capped to once per calendar day per decision (prevents farming stability by repeatedly opening lines).
• lastReviewedAt is NOT updated (it was not active retrieval).
• Never applied to already-due positions (if a prefix move is due, it must be actively tested).

5. The Intra-Session Fail-Forward Loop

In traditional apps, if you get a move wrong, you see the answer and move on. By tomorrow, that line is forgotten again.

ChessSRS enforces an intra-session retry queue:

  1. When you play an incorrect move, the board displays the correct move and highlights the expected square.
  2. The card is immediately appended to the end of your current session's queue.
  3. After you complete the regular due cards, all failed positions re-appear for fresh recall.
  4. A session is not marked "Nothing due" until every lapsed move is recalled cleanly from memory!

6. Review Scopes & Prioritization

The review queue adapts dynamically to user focus:

  • Color Isolation: Top-bar square buttons toggle between White repertoire drawer and Black repertoire drawer. White and Black lines are never mixed in the same session.
  • Opening Scopes: Drill an entire opening family (e.g. "French Defense") or drill a single specific study chapter.
  • Practice Mode: Drill variations freely without modifying your FSRS due dates or stability schedules.
